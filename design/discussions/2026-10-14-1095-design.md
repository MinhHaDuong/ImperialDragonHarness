## Round 1 (summary)

- **A**: `<worktree>/.tmp/`, excluded, purged by roar and `worktree-gc.sh`
  beside `.panel/`. **B**: registered per-task root on `/tmp` with owner file and
  liveness sweep at molt. **C**: one rule plus a report-only top-N of `/tmp`.
- Decisive objections:
  - values and portability: an excluded `.tmp/` is invisible to the WIP rail,
    and `worktree-gc.sh` (l.136-155) and `worktree-exit-preflight.sh` (l.41-52)
    purge *before* the WIP check, so A would delete `.tmp/` even when other WIP
    then blocks removal (confirmed by the probe);
  - evidence: moving bytes to `/home` transfers the risk; the t2093 JSONs are
    evidence; the outages were project-test leaks that A and B do not touch;
  - portability and evidence: B needs liveness it cannot read across hosts and
    slides back to an age rule.

## Design 1: report only ("see it, then decide")

A report-only scan, extending the `session_scratch.scan()` behind healthcheck
item 12. Roots: `${TMPDIR:-/tmp}`, the runtime temp roots, `~/.cache`, and the
`.tmp/` of every registered worktree. Groups by name pattern, ranked by bytes:
`t<N>-*` (labelled "ticket-like name N; ownership unverified"), known runtime
roots, and repeated stems (the project-test-leak detector: `glossary-0882-*`,
`zoteus-*`). Prints use of `/tmp` and `/home`, and `could not read N entries`.
Runs at healthcheck and molt on each host. Deletes nothing.

- **Lifecycle points**: none at command, test or roar; report at molt; crash
  recovery stays manual, informed by the report.
- **Verification**: fixture roots under `tmp_path`, serial; positive controls
  (planted `t9999-x`, twelve `leak-*` siblings, an unreadable entry made
  deterministic rather than mode-000 under root, a fixture `.tmp/`). A null on
  the real host counts only after they pass.
- **Price** (architect): 2 to 3 agent-hours, 0.5 author-hour, about 25 to 45 USD.

| Portability (gpt-6.1-sol) | Portable and useful. Needs: an explicit discovery contract for runtime temp roots; inaccessible roots reported apart from empty ones (Codex sandbox); Pi's real parent temp root located; overlapping roots deduplicated. |

## Design 2: worktree-owned `.tmp/`, removed only with its worktree, plus Design 1

`<worktree>/.tmp/` is declared disposable (logs, caches, venvs, per-command
`TMPDIR=<worktree>/.tmp` for heavy tests, never session-wide because of the
socket-path limit). Evidence never goes there: commits, DVC or the PR. Hunt
appends `/.tmp/` to the common `info/exclude`. **No purge list**: the only thing
that deletes `.tmp/` is the worktree's own removal, after every existing rail
(live cwd, lock, WIP, upstream gone) has passed. Roar preflight and
`worktree-gc.sh` print `.tmp/: N files, X MB` before removing. Sessions with no
worktree: Claude's scratchpad; other runtimes `<primary>/.tmp/`, reported by
Design 1, no lifecycle.

- **Verification**: ordering control (real WIP plus `.tmp/x`: gc skips,
  preflight blocks, `.tmp/x` survives); clean gone tree removed with size line;
  live cwd kept; tracked file under `.tmp/` counts as WIP; no exclude means
  `.tmp/` is WIP; minimum git version pinned; plus Design 1's tests.
- **Price** (architect): 5 to 7 agent-hours, 1 author-hour, about 60 to 100 USD
  (Design 1 included). Values proposes landing it as two PRs, Design 1 first,
  so the report shows the rule's effect.

## Design 3 (author's proposal, after round 2): investigate and report at roar and lair

Proposed by the author when the questions were first presented; framed by the
orchestrator ([d3-spec.md](2026-10-14-1095-seats/d3-spec.md)), then assessed in one
extra round by portability and evidence (values and architect did not sit).

One shared, report-only helper, called from roar and lair:

1. **Scan** Design 1's places (`${TMPDIR:-/tmp}`, `~/.cache` minus known tool
   caches, runtime scratch roots, worktree `.tmp/`), ranked by bytes, with an
   unreadable count.
2. **Investigate** each candidate above a size floor in local session histories
   (Claude `~/.claude/projects`, Pi `~/.pi/agent/sessions`, Codex
   `~/.codex/sessions`), bounded to histories modified after the candidate's
   mtime (a search bound, never a deletion criterion).
3. **Report** path, bytes, the quoted evidence line (history file and line), the
   citing sessions, a liveness state, the linked ticket if any, and citations in
   PR bodies, verdicts and the git log.
4. **The reading session disposes**; existing guards stay. Roar: candidates tied to
   this session and its nested agents. Lair: the whole host, with creators named,
   which covers crash recovery. Per host.

Price: 6 to 8 agent-hours, about 75 to 120 USD, 1 author-hour (orchestrator
estimate; evidence seat finds it plausible).

## Dropped: registered `/tmp` task root (round-1 B)

Dropped by the architect in round 2, with no seat asking to keep it: it needs
cross-host liveness it cannot read, duplicates `session_scratch.py`'s liveness
logic, and drifts back toward an age rule. Its one merit, covering no-worktree
sessions, is served by Design 1's report.

## Agreement

- No age-based deletion of `/tmp`; no deletion keyed on a `tNNNN-*` name (values
  withdrew its own batch-delete idea for this reason).
- Nothing may be purged before the WIP check; an excluded path is invisible to
  that check (measured).
- Project-test leaks (`zoteus-*`, `glossary-0882-*`, parallel pytest) are the
  projects' to fix; the harness names them.
- The `t2093`/`t2094` 11.9G belongs to climate-finance-het; that project decides
  what is evidence. The harness does not sweep it.
- Observability ships first or together; it is the check on any rule.
- Hooks may remind; none is the basis.
- A history hit shows that a session cited a path, not that it created it; a
  miss is not proof of no owner (Design 3 round).

## Remaining disagreements

1. **Is automatic disposal acceptable?** Architect and values (simulated): yes,
   by worktree removal after existing rails. Portability: only once ambiguous
   liveness and evidence retention fail closed.
2. **Roar line: inform or block?** Architect: inform. Evidence and values:
   block, by a mechanical reference check.
3. **Is Design 2 justified before doudou is measured?** Evidence: run Design 1
   on doudou first. Values: land both, Design 1 first. Architect: either.
4. **Design 3 had only two seats.** Portability and evidence approve it as
   reporting; neither the architect nor the values seat assessed it. It contains
   Design 1's scan, so it replaces Design 1 rather than competing with it, and
   it is compatible with Design 2.
