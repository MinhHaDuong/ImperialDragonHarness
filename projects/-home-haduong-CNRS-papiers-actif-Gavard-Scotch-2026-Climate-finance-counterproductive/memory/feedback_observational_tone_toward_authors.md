When a text criticizes other authors' work, as in the Gavard & Schoch comment, Minh wants
non-violent communication. The rules he applied on 2026-09-23:
- Make the article, the estimate or the data the grammatical subject, not "they" or "their". "The
  estimated association can be reproduced", not "Their estimates can be reproduced". "The article
  states…", not "They state…".
- State observations, not verdicts. "The main regression does not distinguish the effect from zero,
  while the introduction states…", not "the claim is not supported by the authors' own regression".
- State the condition under which the authors' argument would hold, rather than dismissing it. For
  example: a disbursement shortfall makes the effect larger "only if the shortfall is the same
  everywhere".
- No motives attributed to third parties. "At a time when aid budgets are under pressure", not "a
  reading donor governments may find convenient".
- Neutral attributions stay ("the authors report / acknowledge"); a comment must say who said what.
- Heavy causal claims about the other authors need exact quotes with pages. Never imply they confuse
  correlation and causation without evidence; he called that "a strong charge".

**Why:** a comment is read by the criticized authors, editors and referees. Judgment words invite a
defensive reply and weaken the scientific posture.

**How to apply:** before sending prose about others' work, sweep it for "they/their", evaluative
verbs ("fails", "not supported", "overstates") and attributed motives, and rephrase them as
observations.
