When drafting prose for the Œconomia manuscript (`content/manuscript.qmd`),
avoid the punchy American self-help / business-book register. The author flagged
these specific tics in a §3.1 draft (2026-06-18): "cash out" / "the abstraction
is cashed out"; "Call X and you mean…" (conversational second person); "The
sharper lesson sits in…"; "This is what X is…"; "where the accounting happens";
hammer-short declaratives used as rhetorical beats.

**Why:** the reviewers and venue are HET/STS academic; this is intellectual
history, not a policy brief or a TED talk. Punchy cadence reads as machine-written
or pop-business and undercuts scholarly credibility.

**How to apply:** measured third-person academic prose. "To describe X as Y is to
say…", "The discrepancy records a normative decision…", "is less a category of
money than a procedure for counting it". Keep concreteness and vivid coinages
(e.g. "electrons have no colour") but frame them as idiom/evidence, not slogans.

`writing.md` Ghost-mode guidance — this is the HET-specific layer.
