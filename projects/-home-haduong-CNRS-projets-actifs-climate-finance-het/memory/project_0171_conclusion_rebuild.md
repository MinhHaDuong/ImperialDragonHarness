Two manuscript-writing disciplines the author enforced, worth reusing:
- **A falsifiable claim must name its falsifier *ex ante* and land *once*.** The
  L&D "test of the thesis" is real only because it says what would prove the
  thesis wrong (a genuine liability/attribution mechanism the mobilisation
  accounts can't absorb) and cites the current evidence (Paris 1/CP.21 §51
  forecloses liability). The anti-pattern ("either outcome confirms me") is the
  STS-commonplace dressed up — the author calls it "se louper". Single-landing:
  the payoff was removed from §3 so it lands only in the conclusion.
- **A conclusion introduces no new facts.** Anchor a conclusion claim only at
  the level the body already establishes. The finer fact (Art 4.3 still funds the
  *agreed full costs of national reports*, Art 12.1) stayed OUT of the conclusion
  because the body only carries "extended, not repealed" (@sec-crystallization);
  and delegate-participation funding was NOT attributed to 1992 (author: no
  source; a Framework Convention states principles, not details).
