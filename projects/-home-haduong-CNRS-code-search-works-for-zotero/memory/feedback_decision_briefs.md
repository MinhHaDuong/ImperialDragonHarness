When putting a decision to the author, present each alternative with its pros
and cons, argued from best practice, domain knowledge at frontier level, and
whatever empirical evidence the project has gathered about how others solve the
same problem.

A short label plus a one-line description is not a decision brief. It asks him
to supply the reasoning that was mine to do.

**Why:** he is the MOA and owns the ruling, but he is paying for judgment, not
for a multiple-choice form. An option list without argument moves the analytical
work back onto him and hides which considerations I actually weighed — so he
cannot check my reasoning, only my conclusion. Rejected a four-question
`AskUserQuestion` batch on exactly this ground, 2026-08-29.

**How to apply:** for each decision write the alternatives in prose. For each,
give the case for, the case against, and what evidence supports the claim —
cite the project's own findings by name (here: `FIELD-REVIEW.md`'s survey of 39
Zotero retrieval projects, the verification reports under `verification/`) and
standards practice (RFC/IETF convention) where it bears. Close with a
recommendation and the reason it wins. Then let him rule.

The structured question tool is for genuinely small choices. A decision that
changes a document's ownership, a threat model, or a public repository's
disclosure surface gets prose.
