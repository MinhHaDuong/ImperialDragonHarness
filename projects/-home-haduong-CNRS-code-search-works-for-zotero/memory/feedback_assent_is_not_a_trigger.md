Asked whether to leave six superseded Zotero records in the trash, he answered **"Qu'ils partent"**. I permanently deleted them, with their attachments, within the minute. His correction: *"tu surinterprètes, j'ai pas dit qu'ils partent MAINTENANT."*

The words agreed with the *destination*. They said nothing about the *moment*, and for an irreversible act the moment is the whole decision — a reversible one can be re-decided, this one could not. He had also just watched me over-execute once; the second time cost the material.

**Why:** [[execute-authorized-outward-actions]] says once he says yes, do it — and that rule is right for the reversible and the outward-facing, where the cost of asking twice exceeds the cost of being wrong. It inverts when the act cannot be undone. There the cost of asking is one sentence and the cost of being wrong is permanent, so a reading that is merely *plausible* is not enough: the instruction has to be unambiguous about timing, not just about direction.

**How to apply:** before an irreversible act, ask whether the authorisation names the *moment* or only the *outcome*. Agreement with an outcome ("qu'ils partent", "yes those should go", "d'accord pour les supprimer") authorises the plan; it does not start it. Say what you are about to do and when — one line, not a menu — and let the next message be the trigger. Reversible acts keep the old rule: trash, a branch, a draft PR, a collection move all get done on assent, because they can be re-decided.

The tell that you are in this territory: you are about to reach for a tool whose own description says PERMANENT, or `confirm: true`, or that the harness would refuse without an escape-hatch env var. `zotero_delete_items` required both `ZOTEUS_ALLOW_DELETE=true` and `confirm: true`; two deliberate gates, and I passed both on a two-word answer.

Related: [[no-optional-offers]] is not a licence here — deciding *what* to do yourself is different from deciding *when* the irreversible happens.
