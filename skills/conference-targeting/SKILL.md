# Conference Targeting

Identify coherent, realistic conference targets for research dissemination—quality over quantity.

## Core Principle

The goal is not to maximize submissions but to identify a **small number of coherent targets** with appropriate formats, minimizing heavy rewrites. Act as an exploration and structuring tool, not a scientific authority.

## Workflow

### 1. Project Characterization

Before searching, understand:
- Nature of contribution (theoretical, methodological, infrastructural, case study)
- Disciplinary positioning (single field vs. interdisciplinary)
- Maturity level (early results, complete study, retrospective)
- Available formats (full paper ready, or needs adaptation)

Ask: "What kind of contribution is this, and what audience would benefit most?"

### 2. Broad Exploration

Search widely across:
- Conferences, workshops, study days (journées d'étude)
- National and international venues
- Multiple related communities

Use web search to find current CFPs. Cast a wide net initially—filtering comes later.

**Search strategy:**
- Query by keywords from the project domain
- Search "[field] conference 2026 CFP"
- Check major association calendars (ACM, IEEE, ADHO, etc.)
- Look for regional/national venues, not just flagship conferences

### 3. CFP Synthesis

For each candidate venue, extract:
- Submission types available (long paper, short paper, poster, demo, abstract)
- Word/page limits per format
- Submission deadline (verify on official site—AI dates may be outdated)
- Conference dates and location
- Estimated participation cost
- Thematic fit with project

**Critical**: Always flag that dates require human verification.

### 4. Fit Assessment

Evaluate alignment on three axes:
- **Thematic**: Does the CFP explicitly welcome this type of work?
- **Format**: Is there a submission type matching the project's current state?
- **Community**: Will reviewers understand and value the contribution?

Score each venue (e.g., 1-5) on these dimensions.

### 5. Format Differentiation

Match format to venue and audience:
- **Oral presentation**: For work needing discussion, SHS-oriented audiences
- **Short paper (4-6 pages)**: For applied results with real data, technical communities
- **Poster**: For infrastructure/tools, enabling direct interaction with practitioners

Different formats for different venues is a feature, not a bug—it adapts the message without heavy rewrites.

### 6. Strategic Prioritization

Recommend 2-4 priority targets with:
- Estimated acceptance probability (be realistic)
- Rationale for fit
- Suggested format
- Key adaptation needed from source material

Conservative vs. ambitious strategies are both valid—make the trade-off explicit.

### 7. Validation Checklist

Before finalizing recommendations:
- [ ] Submission deadlines verified on official sites
- [ ] CFP still open (not past deadline)
- [ ] Format requirements confirmed
- [ ] No conflicts between target conferences
- [ ] Realistic timeline for preparation

## What This Skill Does NOT Do

- Write submission-ready papers (see `conference-submission-prep` skill)
- Guarantee acceptance probabilities
- Replace knowledge of your own field's norms
- Provide authoritative dates (always verify)

## Output Format

Present recommendations as:

```
## Recommended Targets

### 1. [Conference Name] — [Format]
- **Fit**: [Why this venue matches the project]
- **Deadline**: [Date — VERIFY ON SITE]
- **Adaptation needed**: [What changes from source material]
- **Estimated acceptance**: [Realistic assessment]

### 2. ...
```
