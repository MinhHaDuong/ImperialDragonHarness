# Analytical Frameworks Guide

Guide to identifying and characterizing analytical frameworks, methodological debates, and research schools across environmental economics, energy policy, decision science, and related fields.

## Common Framework Patterns

### Pattern 1: Normative vs Positive Approaches

**Normative (prescriptive):** Welfare economics, optimization, "what should be"  
**Positive (descriptive):** Econometric estimation, forecasting, "what is/will be"  
**Integration:** Behavioral economics combines both

### Pattern 2: Partial vs General Equilibrium

**Partial:** Sectoral focus, simpler, more transparent  
**General (CGE):** Economy-wide, captures spillovers, more complex

### Pattern 3: Short-term vs Long-term

**Short:** Current technology, existing institutions  
**Long:** Technological change, institutional evolution, higher uncertainty

### Pattern 4: Deterministic vs Stochastic

**Deterministic:** Scenario analysis  
**Stochastic:** Probability distributions, Monte Carlo, risk analysis

### Pattern 5: Techno-economic vs Socio-technical

**Techno-economic:** Costs, efficiency, optimization  
**Socio-technical:** Includes acceptance, institutions, behavior, systems perspective

## Methodological Archetypes

### Optimization Models
- Objective function (minimize cost, maximize welfare)
- Constraints (technical, resource, policy)
- Used in: Energy planning, integrated assessment, resource management
- Critiques: Unrealistic rationality, ignores barriers

### Simulation Models
- Agent-based or system dynamics
- Heterogeneous actors, emergent behavior
- Used in: Technology diffusion, market dynamics
- Critiques: Many parameters, calibration challenges

### Econometric Analysis
- Statistical estimation, hypothesis testing, causal inference
- Used in: Policy evaluation, demand estimation
- Critiques: Data limitations, endogeneity, extrapolation limits

### Case Studies
- In-depth context-specific analysis
- Qualitative or mixed methods
- Used in: Policy analysis, institutional analysis
- Critiques: Limited generalizability

### Expert Elicitation
- Structured protocols, aggregation methods
- Used in: Long-term forecasting, risk assessment under deep uncertainty
- Critiques: Expert overconfidence, protocol sensitivity

## Research School Mapping

### Identifying Schools

Look for:
- Shared methodological commitments (same modeling platform)
- Co-authorship and citation networks
- Institutional connections
- Common framings

### Example Schools

**Energy systems modelers:** TIMES/MESSAGE platforms, IEA/IIASA institutions  
**Integrated assessment modelers:** DICE/FUND/PAGE, climate-economy coupling  
**Political economy scholars:** Institutional analysis, Sussex SPRU tradition  
**Socio-technical transitions:** Multi-level perspective, deliberative governance

## Theoretical Tensions

### Efficiency vs Equity
Efficiency: Kaldor-Hicks, minimize social cost  
Equity: Distributional impacts, just transitions

### Market Failure vs Government Failure
Market: Externalities require intervention  
Government: Information problems, capture

### Global Optimization vs Local Adaptation
Global: Least-cost worldwide solution  
Local: Context-specific, build on local knowledge

## Scope Conditions Template

For each approach, document:
- **Temporal:** Short/medium/long-term, transient vs steady state
- **Geographical:** Developed/developing, specific regions
- **Sectoral:** Energy supply/demand, transport, industry, buildings
- **Scale:** Micro/meso/macro
- **Key assumptions:** Economic, behavioral, institutional, technical
