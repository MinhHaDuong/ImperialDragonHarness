# J-PAL Grant Proposal Methodology

Detailed guidance for writing competitive grant proposals, based on J-PAL's methodology.

## Core Questions Every Proposal Must Answer

| What? | Why? | How? |
|-------|------|------|
| What is your research question? | Why is your question interesting? | How do you proceed regarding the data? |
| What is new (your contribution)? | Why is the answer consequential? | How do you proceed regarding empirical strategy? |
| What are your expected findings? | Why isn't the answer obvious? | How would this lead to a randomized evaluation? |

## Pre-Proposal Planning

### Step 1: Identify Clear Path to RCT
For J-PAL and RCT-focused funders, must show clear pathway to randomized evaluation.

### Step 2: Check Grant Requirements
- Eligibility requirements
- Selection criteria
- RFP priorities and thematic focus
- Deadlines (expression of interest vs. full proposal)

### Step 3: Check Necessary Approvals
1. **Partner organization review** – Ensure proposal reflects agreement on activities and roles
2. **University review** – Office of research/sponsored programs (allow 2+ weeks)
3. **Collaboration agreements** – Data sharing agreements between institutions if multi-site

### Step 4: Get Letters of Support
Required from:
- Organizations implementing the program
- Organizations helping run evaluation
- Data collection partners

Not needed from consultants/contractors with narrow roles.

## Setting the Scene

### Motivations (Problem Statement)
- Describe challenges in context
- Support with data and prior research
- Emphasize policy relevance and paths to scale

### Research Question
- Directly state the question
- Show fit with literature
- Narrow and specific enough to answer with proposed project
- Respond to eligible themes in RFP

### Contribution to Literature
- Cite relevant papers (peer-reviewed journals, working papers, reputable think tanks)
- State explicitly where gaps are
- Show how your study fills gaps
- Don't disparage prior work—show how you complement it

## Describing the Intervention

### Theory of Change (TOC)
- Describe expected causal chain from intervention to outcomes
- Identify assumptions that must hold
- Identify risks that might prevent realization

**TOC components:**
1. Challenge/problem
2. Intervention design with outputs
3. Immediate outcomes
4. Final outcomes

### Intervention Details
Two components required:
1. **What the intervention is** – What program delivers to recipients
2. **Implementation logistics** – How partner will deliver in context of RCT, why feasible

Show:
- Program is well-designed
- Will plausibly affect outcomes
- Will have sufficient take-up

### For Pilots
Describe preliminary research activities:
- Take-up rate testing
- Logistics practice with partner
- Protocol refinement

## Methodology

### Experimental Design
Specify:
- Number of treatment arms
- Intervention each arm receives
- Unit of randomization
- Sample size
- Randomization procedure (weighted? stratified? phase-in?)

### Hypotheses
State key hypotheses connected to outcomes. Don't need to be as specific as pre-analysis plan, but should specify:
- What outcomes treatment expected to affect
- Where data to measure outcomes comes from

### Outcomes
**If administrative data:**
- How you get access
- What specific measures used
- Reliability of dataset

**If survey data:**
- When and how survey conducted
- By whom (survey firm if known)
- Which questions test each hypothesis

**Timeline:**
- Justify timing of endline
- Why expect results observable in timeframe
- Would results persist longer?

### Mechanisms
Test HOW/WHY treatment causes effects:
- Mediation analysis
- Unbundling treatment components into separate arms
- Subgroup analysis

### Power Calculations
Required for full RCT (not pilots). Must indicate:
1. Minimum detectable effects for intended sample size
2. Assumptions about take-up and compliance
3. Variance of outcome variable
4. Intra-cluster correlation coefficient (ICC)
5. Expected attrition and response rates

Justify assumptions with results from related research.

## Research Team

### Implementing Partners
- Role and experience of organizations running program
- Experience with this type of research
- Demonstrates feasibility

### Research Team
- Relevant experience (especially if not known to funder)
- Previous RCT experience
- Work with implementing partner
- Relevant coursework

## Writing Tips

### Structure
- Build strong structure before writing
- Clear headers and subheaders
- First sentence of paragraph = topic sentence
- New paragraph for each new topic
- No paragraph longer than half page

### Title
Clear and eye-catching. Examples:
- "When Should Governments Subsidize Health? The Case of Mass Deworming"
- "Call Me Maybe: Experimental Evidence on Using Mobile Phones to Survey Microenterprises"

### Literature
- Only cite papers closely linked to your question
- Use your own words
- Don't make negative remarks about prior work
- "We complement these approaches by..." not "The problem with these papers is..."

### Language
- Avoid superlatives ("very", "extremely", "highly")
- Write in active voice
- Don't start sentences with "this" without the noun
- Check paragraph breaks—one idea per paragraph

### Review Process
- Re-read multiple times
- Read paragraph backwards for grammar
- Ask field expert to review substance
- Ask good writer to review clarity
