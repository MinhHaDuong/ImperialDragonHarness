# Reading note template

The single template for reading notes, whether written alone or delegated by
`critical-lit-review`. Keep the section headers verbatim: notes are compared
and searched across a corpus. Target 1000–1500 words excluding quotations;
Summary and Critical analysis carry roughly equal weight.

# Reading note: <work title>

## Summary

- **Question and approach**: what the work asks, how it goes about it.
- **Main arguments or findings**: three to five, in your own words.
- **Evidence and method**: sources, data, analytical procedure.
- **Concepts and framework**: theory mobilized, central concepts.

## Critical analysis

- **Strengths**: what it contributes, what it does well.
- **Limitations and blind spots**: what is unexamined, which questions are not
  asked, which actors, cases or data are absent.
- **Position in debates**: the conversation it joins; whom it agrees and
  disagrees with; the gap it claims.
- **Assumptions**
  - *Explicit*: acknowledged starting points.
  - *Implicit*: taken for granted without discussion.
  - *Naturalized*: presented as technical or self-evident when contingent or
    political.
- **Innovations**: new concepts, tools or methods it introduces, if any.

## Research relevance

- **Connection to the guiding question**: framework, methodological model,
  empirical precedent, or a position in a controversy.
- **Analytical axes informed**: which of the project's axes it illuminates.
- **Controversy clarified**: the debate or tension it helps map.
- **Gaps revealed**: lacunae the author's research could address.

## Key quotations

> "Exact quotation." (p. N)

Why it matters, one line. Two to four quotations; page read on the page.

## Notes for future writing

Where and how to cite it; which argument it supports or opposes.
